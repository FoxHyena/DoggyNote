# Artwork-first sticker browsing

User-requested design review, implementation and QA, 2026-10-20. This review covers the tray and enlarged preview; the existing circular canvas manipulation remains unchanged. Routine interactive QA uses the Paseo browser, with Playwright for cross-engine and touch regressions. No native controls or new native approval prompts were used.

## Design decision

Let the artwork be the grid. Each tile keeps its click/tap-to-place and desktop drag behavior, with no repeated Preview caption or second button. Preview is a contextual action: hold on touch or with a pointer, right-click on desktop, or focus a tile and press Space / Shift+F10. Enter retains placement. One quiet tray-level hint explains preview access. This trades persistent per-item affordances for more visible artwork; keyboard descriptions, shortcuts and a pointer tooltip supplement the shared hint.

The normal header is simply **Stickers**, with Close. Removed “A little personality, anywhere” from every header state. The enlarged view has a Back control, **Preview** title and Close. Its content follows the user's decisions in order:

1. See the sticker at a useful size, without a redundant emoji caption.
2. Recognize its source in one full-width row: a subdued “From sticker pack” label, the real pack title and a chevron. The whole row opens that pack. Titles wrap rather than collide with actions.
3. Act in a stable bottom row: **Place sticker** is the primary button; a framed star is the secondary Favorite/Unfavorite action. The star retains its accessible name, tooltip, pressed state and 44px target. Place and Favorite remain disabled until the pack is added.

Pack provenance is navigation, so it sits with metadata rather than competing as a third button beside Place and the star. Emoji remain useful for search and accessible tile names. Back restores the originating tile's focus. Opening a source pack from the collection now offers **Your collection** as its return route; importing from Add retains **Add a pack**.

At short window heights, artwork and source sit side by side with actions below. Landscape touch browsing shares the header row with search and uses eight columns, preserving usable visible artwork instead of a few oversized, mostly clipped cells. Portrait uses the existing four-column grid and bottom sheet.

## Telegram evidence and adaptation

The user's iOS screenshots show an artwork-only picker and a contextual message menu with Favorites and View Sticker Set. They support separating browsing from secondary actions. No live Telegram app was controlled.

Read the primary [Telegram Desktop sticker picker source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/telegramdesktop/tdesktop/dev/Telegram/SourceFiles/chat_helpers/stickers_list_widget.cpp): its preview timer opens a held sticker, and release after a shown preview does not choose/send it. The official [iOS translation record](https://translations.telegram.org/en/ios/settings/StickerPacksSettings.ManagingHelp) also describes holding a sticker message and opening its pack. These are source/translation evidence, not a claim that every current Telegram client behaves identically.

DoggyNote adapts the contextual preview to board placement: inspect first, then explicitly choose Place and a board location. Keeping the art visible and grouping secondary actions follows the supplied reference without copying a message-specific menu into a board tool.

## Findings and fixes

| Finding | Fix and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Preview text under every sticker doubled the grid's controls and reduced room for art | Removed the repeated controls. Functional tests assert their absence and verify contextual preview without placement. |
| Emoji, short pack identifier and unrelated buttons competed in the preview | Dedicated source row uses the real pack title; primary placement and icon-only Favorite have a separate action row. |
| Returning from a source pack misleadingly pointed to Add | Remember the originating mode and offer the appropriate return route; contextual navigation regression returns to the current collection. |
| Landscape touch tiles were clipped enough that a tile's geometric center was outside its visible area | Compact landscape chrome and eight columns expose usable art. Touch QA presses within the visible intersection and requires at least 44px of visible height. |
| Chromium could turn a successful landscape hold into armed placement on release | Event instrumentation proved pointerdown targeted the tile, then pointerup and click targeted the newly mounted Place button at the same coordinates. A native capture listener suppresses that trailing click. A new pointerdown or keyboard action removes the guard immediately; no timeout delays the next deliberate action. |

The first redesign-focused run passed 29 and failed both phone landscape checks. After layout cleanup, WebKit passed but Chromium still failed; changing the test tray-opening helper to real taps did not fix it. A targeted event trace identified the real release-click retargeting above. After the fix, all six focused contextual, keyboard and phone cases passed with retries disabled. Both phones assert the preview stays open, no placement is armed or committed by release, and the next deliberate Place tap works.

## Mobile bottom-right sticker shelf experiment

The user requested a bottom-right sticker icon that opens a slot for browsing and dragging onto the board. The implemented phone-only launcher sits above the existing bottom dock beside the zoom controls, so ordinary board creation/navigation remain available. Its pressed/expanded appearance ties it to the shelf. It is hidden during note editing and armed placement, and stays outside selected-sticker controls.

The collection becomes a single horizontal row of artwork, with pack tabs, local search and Add pack above it. A partial next sticker provides a browsing cue. Horizontal swipes browse; an upward pull beyond a direction threshold lifts a still-image ghost clear of the finger. Release over the canvas commits once; release in the shelf cancels. Tap still arms deliberate placement and holding still opens the existing larger preview. Per-pack horizontal position is retained alongside desktop vertical position. Import and large-preview modes retain their fuller layouts.

The first review found that the shelf class accidentally applied to enlarged previews, collapsing their header, and that gesture movement was coupled to the cancelled long-press timer. Restricted shelf layout to active collection browsing and track pressed movement independently from that timer. A test capture also exposed default touch highlight/hover artifacts on the launcher; replaced these with theme-based expanded feedback and disabled the browser tap highlight for this button. Tests switch theme with the tray closed so Escape does not intentionally dismiss the tray instead of the account menu.

Focused phone WebKit/Chromium verification passed 4/4 with retries disabled after the preview/gesture fixes. Actually inspected round-21 light WebKit shelf, dark Chromium shelf, landscape WebKit shelf and Chromium lifted ghost. The shelf leaves a visible board destination above; artwork and pack selection are readable, and the source/Place/star preview layout remains intact. Latest full checks and captures after the launcher-feedback cleanup are recorded in README.md.

The expanded round-21 review caught a concrete landscape collision: the new launcher remained above the larger preview and covered its Favorite star. Hide the launcher while a full preview/import/empty-state surface is open; that surface already has Close. Added a landscape assertion that the launcher is hidden and an actual touch Favorite toggle must succeed. Fresh captures after this fix are required before accepting the preview layout.

A subsequent capture showed inherited generic icon-button pressed styles dimming the launcher. Gave it dedicated button styling; the round-24 light WebKit and dark Chromium captures now show clear accent feedback. The related phone flows and the three full-suite fixture failures passed27 repeated cases with retries disabled; full final regression remains recorded in README.md.

The final gesture review found a center mismatch in the new shelf drag: its ghost was offset56 CSS pixels above the finger, but release used the finger's coordinate. Share the offset constant between ghost rendering and touch-drop conversion, so placement preserves the visible preview center. Added actual touch-drag assertions comparing the ghost center before release to the placed object's center afterward. Tap-to-place and desktop placement keep their existing coordinate behavior. The preceding full run was stopped cleanly after306 passes because its served build predated this fix; it is not counted as complete verification.
